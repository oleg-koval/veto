A Short History of Rome

Founding (753 BC)

According to legend, Rome was founded by Romulus on the Palatine Hill in 753 BC. The early city was ruled by a series of kings.

The Republic (509–27 BC)

After overthrowing the monarchy, Rome became a republic governed by elected magistrates and the Senate. It expanded across Italy and then the Mediterranean, defeating Carthage in the Punic Wars.

The Empire (27 BC–476 AD)

Augustus became the first emperor in 27 BC, beginning the Pax Romana — two centuries of relative peace and prosperity. The empire reached its greatest extent under Trajan, stretching from Britain to the Middle East.

Decline and Fall

Internal strife, economic troubles, and invasions by Germanic tribes weakened the empire. The Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when Romulus Augustulus was deposed. The Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire continued for another thousand years until 1453.

Legacy

Rome's laws, language (Latin), architecture, and governance shaped Western civilization, influencing modern legal systems, languages, and engineering.
