A Short History of Berlin

Founding (13th century)

Berlin emerged as a trading settlement on the Spree river, first documented around 1237. It grew alongside the neighboring town of Cölln, with which it later merged.

Capital of Prussia (1701–1871)

Berlin became the capital of the Kingdom of Prussia in 1701. Under rulers like Frederick the Great, it grew into a center of culture, military power, and the Enlightenment in Germany.

Capital of a Unified Germany (1871–1933)

With German unification in 1871, Berlin became the imperial capital. The Weimar era that followed World War I turned it into a hub of art, science, and nightlife, despite economic and political turmoil.

Nazi Era and World War II (1933–1945)

Berlin became the capital of Nazi Germany in 1933. The city was heavily bombed during World War II and fell to Soviet forces in 1945, leaving much of it in ruins.

Divided City (1945–1989)

After the war, Berlin was split into East and West sectors controlled by the Allies and the Soviet Union. The Berlin Wall, built in 1961, became the most visible symbol of the Cold War, dividing the city until it fell in 1989.

Reunification and Today

Germany reunified in 1990, and Berlin was restored as the national capital in 1999. Today it is a major European cultural, political, and economic center, known for its history, art scene, and resilience.
