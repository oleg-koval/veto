A Brief History of New York

Originally inhabited by the Lenape Native American tribe, New York was first visited by Europeans when Giovanni da Verrazzano, an Italian explorer, arrived in 1524.

In 1609, Henry Hudson, working for the Dutch East India Company, sailed up the Hudson River, leading to Dutch colonization. The Dutch named the area New Amsterdam.

In 1664, the British took control of New Amsterdam and renamed it New York, after the Duke of York. The city became a significant commercial center.

During the 18th century, New York played a crucial role in the American Revolution. It was a key location for significant events and battles.

By the 19th century, New York had become one of the most populous cities in the United States, driven by immigration and industrialization.

The 20th century saw New York become a global financial and cultural hub, with iconic landmarks such as the Statue of Liberty and the Empire State Building.
